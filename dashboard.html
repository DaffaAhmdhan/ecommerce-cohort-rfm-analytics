E-Commerce Cohort Retention & RFM Segmentation

Evaluating Rp 4.18B in multi-category order volume across 12 months (Nov 2025 – Oct 2026) to measure cohort decay, quantify revenue concentration via RFM quantiles, and isolate the review penalty caused by late shipping.

Analyst Field Notes

Where does the money—and churn risk—live? Just 289 Champions (12.0% of customers) drive 30.7% of total revenue (Rp 1.28B), averaging 5.48 orders every 37.8 days. On the risk side, 274 At-Risk customers represent Rp 432.6M in historical value who haven't returned in an average of 252.6 days. Most critically, when an order misses its delivery SLA (avg 8.02 days vs 3.49 days on-time), the average rating collapses from 4.25★ to 2.45★—pushing 1–2 star reviews from 6.38% to 55.1%.

SQL & Python Quantile RFM Methodology (from sql/cohort_and_rfm.sql & src/run_analysis.py) 
-- 1. Customer-level Recency, Frequency & Monetary aggregation
SELECT
    customer_id,
    CAST(julianday('2026-11-01') - julianday(MAX(order_date)) AS INTEGER) AS recency_days,
    COUNT(order_id) AS frequency,
    SUM(order_value_idr) AS monetary_idr
FROM orders
GROUP BY customer_id;

-- 2. Delivery SLA Impact on Review Score
SELECT
    CASE WHEN is_late_delivery = 1 THEN 'Late Delivery' ELSE 'On-Time Delivery' END AS delivery_status,
    COUNT(*) AS order_count,
    ROUND(AVG(actual_delivery_days), 2) AS avg_delivery_days,
    ROUND(AVG(review_score), 2) AS avg_review_score,
    ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN review_score <= 2 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) / COUNT(*), 2) AS low_rating_pct
FROM orders
GROUP BY is_late_delivery;
