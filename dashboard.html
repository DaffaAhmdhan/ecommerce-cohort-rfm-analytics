E-Commerce Cohort Retention & RFM Segmentation

Evaluating Rp 4.18B in multi-category order volume across 12 months (Nov 2025 – Oct 2026) to measure cohort decay, identify top-value buyer groups via RFM quantiles, and isolate the review penalty caused by late shipping.

Key Takeaway (In Plain English)

Where does the money—and churn risk—live? Just 289 Champions (12.0% of customers) drive 30.7% of total revenue (Rp 1.28B), averaging 5.48 orders every 37.8 days. Meanwhile, 274 At-Risk buyers represent Rp 432.6M in past spend who haven't ordered in 252.6 days. Most importantly, when delivery misses its promised SLA (avg 8.02 days vs 3.49 days on-time), average ratings crash from 4.25★ to 2.45★—spiking 1–2 star reviews from 6.38% to 55.1%.

SQL & Python Quantile RFM Methodology (from sql/cohort_and_rfm.sql) 
-- 1. Customer-level Recency, Frequency & Monetary aggregation
SELECT
    customer_id,
    CAST(julianday('2026-11-01') - julianday(MAX(order_date)) AS INTEGER) AS recency_days,
    COUNT(order_id) AS frequency,
    SUM(order_value_idr) AS monetary_idr
FROM orders
GROUP BY customer_id;

-- 2. Delivery SLA Impact on Review Score
SELECT
    CASE WHEN is_late_delivery = 1 THEN 'Late Delivery' ELSE 'On-Time Delivery' END AS delivery_status,
    COUNT(*) AS order_count,
    ROUND(AVG(actual_delivery_days), 2) AS avg_delivery_days,
    ROUND(AVG(review_score), 2) AS avg_review_score,
    ROUND(100.0 * SUM(CASE WHEN review_score <= 2 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) / COUNT(*), 2) AS low_rating_pct
FROM orders
GROUP BY is_late_delivery;
